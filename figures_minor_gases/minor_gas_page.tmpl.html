What the CH₄, C₂H₆, NH₃ and CO test cases exercise

Each case puts one gas in a nitrogen atmosphere on the TS273K profile (300 levels), at a present-Earth-like and an elevated abundance, under the Sun and a 3400 K blackbody. Every panel compares the committed baseline against the same column with the gas removed, so the bands each k-table acts in are visible. Model: exort.exe, 84-band grid, HITRAN-2024.

Change relative to a pure-N₂ column

Bars are scaled within each column. OLR is the same under both stars; the N₂-only reference gives OLR W m⁻², and surface SW↓ W m⁻² (Sun / 3400 K).

What a baseline proves

These are the model's own outputs, frozen as regression baselines. They catch any change to the CH₄/C₂H₆/NH₃/CO paths (every case moves some output by more than 3× the 1e-3 tolerance), but they are not a line-by-line validation.

Spectral densities

Per-band flux divided by band width (cm⁻¹ for the longwave, µm for the shortwave), so band shapes compare across the uneven 84-band grid. Each density integrates back to the broadband flux, and the plotting script checks this.

Heating rates

Shown in K day⁻¹, the unit RTprofile_out.nc stores them in. The large SW heating near the model top in the elevated cases is gas absorption with no other coolant present.
